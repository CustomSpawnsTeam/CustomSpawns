# Invasion logistics

An optional, element-only `Invasion` block in a spawn definition enables invasion occupation and logistics. `RequiresFirstCoastalConquest` separately opts that spawn into coastal-first targeting. Omitting the `Invasion` block preserves existing spawn behaviour. The block belongs to the spawn, not its shared party-behaviour template.

```xml
<Invasion>
  <RequiresFirstCoastalConquest>true</RequiresFirstCoastalConquest>
  <SupplyLine>Coastal</SupplyLine>
  <MinimumCapitalGarrison>400</MinimumCapitalGarrison>
  <MinimumFiefGarrison>250</MinimumFiefGarrison>
  <LordPartySizeBonus>50</LordPartySizeBonus>
  <Conquest>
    <ThreatPerFiefHeld>0.05</ThreatPerFiefHeld>
    <OpenFrontIntervalInDays>30</OpenFrontIntervalInDays>
    <TargetSiegePreference>4</TargetSiegePreference>
    <ReconsiderTargetIntervalInDays>30</ReconsiderTargetIntervalInDays>
  </Conquest>
  <MaxBankedTroops>1700</MaxBankedTroops>
  <BankedTroopsPerTownHeld>300</BankedTroopsPerTownHeld>
  <Supplies>
    <FoodPerConvoy>200</FoodPerConvoy>
    <IntervalInDays>7</IntervalInDays>
  </Supplies>
  <Reinforcements>
    <Rule>
      <Id>infantry_wave</Id>
      <PartyTemplateId>cs_makko_invasion_reinforcement</PartyTemplateId>
      <IntervalInDays>7</IntervalInDays>
      <LordsPerWave>3</LordsPerWave>
      <MaxMenPerWave>600</MaxMenPerWave>
    </Rule>
    <Rule>
      <Id>cavalry_wave</Id>
      <PartyTemplateId>cs_makko_invasion_horse</PartyTemplateId>
      <IntervalInDays>21</IntervalInDays>
    </Rule>
  </Reinforcements>
</Invasion>
```

These are illustrative values, not a guarantee of rebellion prevention or siege success. No spawn definition in this repository currently carries an `Invasion` block, so the numbers above are a starting point to balance against, not shipped balance. Scouts, ordinary armies, original spawn chances/caps and shared behaviour templates are unchanged.

## Configuration

| Element | Meaning / omitted default |
| --- | --- |
| `RequiresFirstCoastalConquest` | When `true`, restricts the invasion to coastal towns while its current faction owns no coastal town; default `false`. |
| `SupplyLine` | Where the invasion's supplies may come from: `Coastal`, `Any` or `None`; omitted, supplies come by sea, as they did before there was a choice. `Coastal` makes a coastal town the only supply base, `Any` any town, and `None` removes the dependency altogether — nothing has to be retaken for supply's sake. Supply decides which capture winds the reinforcement clocks and whether the first-coastal-conquest restriction applies; it never decides whether reinforcements come, which turns on the capital alone. |
| `MinimumCapitalGarrison` | How many men the invasion keeps standing in its capital: the floor its lords draw that garrison down to but never below; default 0. It says nothing about any other town the invasion holds. |
| `MinimumFiefGarrison` | How many men every other fief the invasion's clans hold is garrisoned with; default 0, which leaves fiefs with whatever they were left. Once a day the thinnest fief that is not besieged is topped up first, out of what the capital holds above `MinimumCapitalGarrison`, with the same proportional mix of troops a lord draws; a fief without a garrison is given one. While any of these fiefs stands short, the invasion's lords lay no new siege: vanilla's siege scores are zeroed for every settlement except the capital and the fiefs of the kingdom being conquered, so retaking the capital is never held back and the conquest never stalls once the invasion's waves stop at its strength limit and its bank runs dry. Without a held capital nothing is gated. |
| `LordPartySizeBonus` | How many more men each lord of the invasion may lead than vanilla's party size limit gives him; default 0. Only a lord's own party grows, and only while his invasion is still fighting. Vanilla sizes a lord's party by his clan's tier alone, which left the invasion's lords at about a hundred men each against a capital bank of thousands; the bonus raises what each lord draws from the bank, while the number of lords still only grows with the towns the invasion holds (`LordsPerTownHeld`). |
| `Conquest` | Opts the invasion into a war of conquest; omitted, the diplomacy configuration alone decides its wars. The first fief the invasion takes from a kingdom names that kingdom its target, and the invasion stays at war with it until it falls; it then turns on the best prey on its borders. Every other kingdom's war and peace with the invasion is left to vanilla diplomacy instead of the diplomacy configuration. |
| `FollowsInvasion` | The raw party template ID (no `PartyTemplate.` prefix) of the invasion this one is launched for; omitted, the invasion comes on its own. Such a spawn is never rolled by chance: it is launched when a kingdom falls to the invasion it follows or to one of that one's followers, lands far from them and conquers a kingdom of its own, at peace and allied with them. Several spawns may follow one invasion; each fallen kingdom launches the next. It needs its own party template and spawn clan, so the realms stay apart. Followers are optional: once a line has none left to send, it launches copies of its first invasion on its own. See [Conquest and threat](#conquest-and-threat). |
| `Conquest/ThreatPerFiefHeld` | The share of the invasion's settlement worth that each fief it holds adds to another kingdom's score for declaring war on it, and takes off its score for making peace with it; default 0, no threat beyond vanilla's. Vanilla proposes a war once the score passes a sixth of that worth, so `0.05` makes a neutral neighbour declare at about four fiefs. |
| `Conquest/OpenFrontIntervalInDays` | The fewest days between two wars the invasion declares itself; default 0, never. |
| `Conquest/TargetSiegePreference` | How many times more the invasion's lords want to besiege a fief of the kingdom being conquered, until it falls; any such siege they can win is also wanted above every other siege. Default 0, and anything up to 1 leaves their sieges to vanilla. |
| `Conquest/ReconsiderTargetIntervalInDays` | How often the invasion weighs its target against the other kingdoms on its borders, turning on one that has become a much better prey (`Invasion conquest: reconsidered`). Default 0 keeps the target until it falls. |
| `Conquest/GarrisonArmy` | Leads part of the capital's garrison out as one army against the target kingdom every so often; omitted, no such army is raised. `IntervalInDays` is the fewest days between two armies (default 90, minimum 1), and `GarrisonShare` the share of the garrison an army takes at most (default 0.5, at most 1). An army takes the smaller of `GarrisonShare` times the garrison and the men above `MinimumCapitalGarrison`: with 0.5 and a minimum of 400, a 3000-man garrison sends 1500 and a 10000-man one 5000. See [Conquest and threat](#conquest-and-threat). |
| `MaxBankedTroops` | How many men the capital's garrison, the troop bank, may hold before waves stop bringing men; shared by all of the invasion's rules; default 0, which leaves the bank bottomless. |
| `BankedTroopsPerTownHeld` | What every town the invasion's kingdom holds right now adds to that limit; default 0. The capital is a town, so `1700` plus `300` is a 2000-man limit while the invasion holds only its capital. The bonus is the kingdom's, not the invading clans': a town held by an allied clan of the same kingdom raises the limit as much as one the invasion took itself. Read live, so a town lost stops counting the moment it changes hands. |
| `Supplies/FoodPerConvoy` | How much food each convoy unloads into the capital's own market; default 0, which runs no convoys at all and leaves the capital feeding itself. Size it against what the capital's garrison eats, since that garrison is the invasion's troop bank and is the reason the town needs more food than it produces. |
| `Supplies/IntervalInDays` | How often a convoy arrives; default 7, minimum 1. Its own cadence — changing how often the invasion is reinforced leaves its supplies alone, and the other way round. Ignored when no food is configured. |
| `Reinforcements/Rule` | One reinforcement stream. At least one is required; declare as many as the invasion needs, and they draw in the order written. |
| `Reinforcements/Rule/Id` | Required non-blank name, unique within the invasion. It names the rule's own clock, so editing or reordering the configuration does not move a running schedule onto another rule. |
| `Reinforcements/Rule/PartyTemplateId` | Required raw troop-template ID, without the `PartyTemplate.` prefix. Each rule brings its own. |
| `Reinforcements/Rule/IntervalInDays` | Interval for both the first and later waves of this rule; default 7, minimum 1. Waves are considered once a day, so a shorter interval is refused rather than silently rounded. |
| `Reinforcements/Rule/MaxWaves` | How many waves this rule ever sends; default 0, which leaves it sending for as long as the invasion lives. One wave plus an interval of one day is the one-off reinforcement: it arrives the first day after the capital falls and nothing follows it. Only waves actually paid into the garrison count, so one refused for a besieged capital or a full bank is sent an interval later instead of being lost. |
| `Reinforcements/Rule/LordsPerWave` | How many new lords join the invasion with each wave of this rule; default 0, a wave of troops only. Each fills his party out of the capital's garrison. |
| `Reinforcements/Rule/MaxMenPerWave` | How many men one wave of this rule banks; default 0, the template's whole roll. The template is rolled again until there are that many, and the total is cut down with every kind of troop cut by the same share, so the template's mix is kept. Three lords drawing about 150 each out of a 600-man wave leave about 150 in the bank. |
| `Reinforcements/Rule/MenPerTownHeld` | Men a wave banks over `MaxMenPerWave` for every town the invasion holds, its capital included, the way `BankedTroopsPerTownHeld` and `LordsPerTownHeld` count them; default 0. With 800 and 300, a wave brings 1100 men while only the capital is held and 1400 with a second town. It does nothing without `MaxMenPerWave`. |

All quantities must be finite and nonnegative. The bank limit counts men, wounded included, in the capital's garrison only: field parties and other fiefs' garrisons do not fill it. Occupation dumps the whole escort roster into the captured town's garrison, so the bank starts at whatever the landing brought rather than at `MaxBankedTroops`. A strength ceiling over the whole faction let the bank grow to several thousand men, a capital no army would ever besiege, so an invasion that ran out of momentum could never be finished; the bank limit keeps the capital takeable. Runtime validation checks each rule's troop-template reference, non-bandit ownership and an effective behaviour template able to besiege towns. Invalid opt-ins are diagnosed without breaking unrelated valid spawn definitions.

A clan may be invaded for once: one faction, one invasion. The clan an invasion invades for is what identifies it — it holds the towns taken, fields the parties and carries the kingdom left behind — so the invaded clan must be settled by the configuration and unique across it. An invasion spawn therefore cannot use `OverridenClanSpawns`, which draws a clan at random on every spawn, nor `InheritClanFromSettlement`, which would make the invading faction whoever owns the landing site; and a second invasion spawn naming a clan an earlier one already invades for is reported and ignored rather than merged into it, because a invasion reads one spawn template's configuration and merging would silently discard the other's. None of this limits what one invasion can do over time: further waves are the same spawn firing again, which join the existing invasion alongside whatever parties are still stranded from an earlier attempt, and several troop streams at once are what the reinforcement rules are for. Lords brought by a wave inherit the original `PriorityPartyBehaviourTemplateId` along with the rest of the invasion; there is no second behaviour configuration.

## Invasions, conquests and supply

One logistics invasion belongs to an original spawn-template ID and the clan that founded it, plus any further clan it expands into when that one runs out of war party slots. Several original armies and their escorts can share it. Each town becomes a foothold of the verified capturing party's invasion alone; allied conquests gain no duplicate foothold or supply.

When `RequiresFirstCoastalConquest` is enabled and the invasion party's current faction owns no coastal town, the invasion may begin a siege only at an eligible coastal town with a reachable land gate. Existing hostility, defender-strength, reconnaissance and siege-compatibility checks still apply. No viable coast means the normal raid/patrol fallback, not an inland exception. Until the invasion has taken a town anywhere, owning any coastal town unlocks the configured town/castle targets. The first town it captures becomes its capital. While the invasion holds that capital the configured targets are unlocked; losing it starts a recapture objective aimed at the capital first, with the timed alternative-target rules described under Losing the foothold. Which town the capital is, is saved with the invasion; whether it is still held is read from live ownership.

Ownership loss deletes the foothold and pending occupation effects. Peaceful reacquisition does not restore them. A new attributable siege capture creates a new transition. Assault victories and winning besieger-side sally-out results use native ownership handling; successful occupation promotes the capturing invasion clan into its own kingdom.

An invasion supplies its capital by convoy, and by nothing else. Every `IntervalInDays` a convoy unloads `FoodPerConvoy` worth of grain into the capital's own market, and the town then lives off it exactly as it lives off anything a trader brings in: hunger, loyalty and rebellion stay vanilla's to work out, and nothing writes a food balance or grants a town an allowance. That is the whole of it — no other town the invasion holds is supplied, and a town it takes and does not make its capital lives on what it produces, like any other town that changes hands.

This replaces the food support the invasion used to have, which stocked a captured town once and then fed it daily. A flat allowance either left a conquest short, which dropped its loyalty and invited the rebellion the support was meant to prevent, or was generous enough that nothing the invasion held ever ran short at all. A convoy has neither problem: it is a quantity of real food arriving at a known rate, so a capital whose garrison outgrows the convoy goes hungry on vanilla's terms, and the men in the bank are what the hunger is charged against.

The first convoy is due an interval after the capital falls, not on the day of it, because the clock is wound by the capture rather than spent by it. While the capital is lost or besieged the clock holds where it is instead of running on — a convoy cannot be unloaded into a town the invasion cannot reach, and a siege is already starving that town by vanilla's own rules — so whatever was due is unloaded once the way in is open again. That is deliberately the opposite of a reinforcement wave, which spends a slot it could not deliver: a wave refused is one the invasion can afford to lose, and a convoy refused is food it was going to need either way.

What a foothold's supply status still decides is which capture winds the reinforcement clocks, and which settlement the invasion is sent back to retake when it loses one.

## Occupation and reinforcement lifecycle

Occupation waits until ownership and the actual battle participant snapshot agree, the native garrison is active, and the town and donors have left their map events. One guarded hourly operation merges the escorts into the garrison, then records completion. Duplicate callbacks and save/load must not repeat the package.

The transfer is unconditional: every still-active escort party among the capture's recorded participants has its whole `MemberRoster` added to the town's garrison, and the emptied party is then destroyed. There is no configured or capacity-derived bound on the resulting garrison, and no filtering of wounded troops or heroes — the roster moves as it stands. Non-escort participants, parties that are no longer active and other groups are excluded. Native wages and recruitment remain in force. Native wage and party-size desertion do not apply to an invasion's garrisons (see below); morale desertion does.

Every rule's first wave is due at the capture that gave the invasion its capital plus that rule's own interval, and each rule runs its own clock from there, so a stream refused for a full bank never holds the others off. A daily check considers each due rule once. A wave has one destination: the capital's own garrison, which is the invasion's troop bank. The rule's troop template is rolled once per kind of troop in it, between the numbers the template declares, and paid straight in, cut to `MaxMenPerWave` when the roll is larger — nothing marches across the map, no party is created, and the garrison takes the wave whole however close to its limit the bank is. The rule's `LordsPerWave` lords are then generated into the invasion's clans and field their parties at the capital, drawing their men back out of that same garrison down to `MinimumCapitalGarrison` and no further. Each withdrawal takes a proportional share of every non-hero troop type in the garrison. Fractional shares are rounded to fill the requested size, so successive lords and the capital retain a similar mix of recruits, veterans and specialists. A lord beaten and come back draws the same way, which is what makes the bank the invasion's real strength: an empty bank sends him out alone to recruit Calradians. A wave held back because the bank is full still sends its lords, up to the lord cap, but not its men (`Invasion lords fielded at full strength`): the bank already holds what they need, and without them a full bank would sit untouched once the lords it has are captured.

Nothing arrives unless the invasion holds its capital and that capital is not under siege. An invasion that has yet to take a capital, or that has lost the one it took, is sent nothing and fights on with what it has; a besieged capital is cut off both ways, so no wave is paid in and no lord takes any out either. The clock runs through all of it — a wave refused is not banked up to be paid later — but a refused wave does not count against `MaxWaves`, so a rule allowed only so many waves still owes the ones it could not deliver.

All of an invasion's rules draw against one shared bank limit, which is the only valve on the bank. A wave is banked whenever the bank is below its limit, so it can overshoot by at most one wave, which the wave's own lords then draw back down. When several rules come due on the same day they are served in configuration order, each decided only after the one before it has been paid in, so the first takes the room and a later one can find none left and wait for its next interval. The limit itself is never saved: it is recomputed from the configuration and the towns the invasion's kingdom holds at that moment.

Two paths hand an invasion clan an ordinary lord party under a name no spawn template matches, so that it carries on outside its invasion. The common one runs all campaign: a spawned party is handed to its clan leader as owner, and unless a lord is generated to lead it that clan leader stays its owner without ever standing in a roster, so the game reads it as having no party and fields one for it whenever the clan sits under its tier party limit. A generated besieging lord is not that hero — it both owns and leads the party it was made for, and is enrolled in its roster. The other is a living invasion party changing component, as one does when the invasion becomes a kingdom and loses its only coastal town. Either party is adopted back into its clan's invasion: it is registered under that invasion's spawn template and carries the `"Adopted"` role across saves. That restores its membership, steering and spawn-sized party limit. Its roster is left as the game filled it: adoption restores the invasion identity the party lost, not the troops standing in it.

Adoption counts nothing and refuses nothing: any lord party of a clan the invasion holds is taken back, however many the invasion already fields. There is no need to count, because the invasion's own clan membership already says which parties are its, and how many men it may field is the bank's question, through the bank limit and the lord cap. Garrisons, caravans and militias of the same clan are not lord parties and are never adopted, and a party already registered keeps the identity it was registered with.

A due slot blocked by an unavailable capital, a full bank, or a configuration or troop template that has changed under it is skipped until that rule's next interval. There is no backlog or catch-up loop; advancing multiple weeks produces at most one current wave per rule. Capital ownership and siege are rechecked at the moment the wave would be paid in. Membership, the per-rule deadlines and the waves each rule has already sent all persist. A clock whose rule the configuration no longer declares spends its due slot once and banks nothing, rather than being retried daily. Losing the original army does not stop an eligible surviving foothold; a invasion disappears only when it has neither a foothold nor surviving members and the kingdom it leaves behind has been retired.

## Strength monitoring

Search the module log for `Invasion strength summary:`. Each invasion reports once on the first campaign tick after loading a save and once per campaign day after reinforcement delivery and capital refills. The entry includes campaign day, kingdom, invasion-clan count, towns/castles held, living lords, total estimated force strength, the capital bank's troops, limit and headroom (`bank_troops`, `bank_limit`, `headroom`), the conquered kingdom's strength, field-party count and strength, total/healthy men, average/minimum/maximum party size, average party limit, overall fill percentage, garrison totals and capital siege/garrison status. A bottomless bank's headroom appears as the largest integer.

`faction_strength` is the sum of active war parties and fief garrisons belonging to the invasion's clans; it includes the original spawned forces. `bank_troops` is the capital garrison's head count, the exact value the bank limit is checked against. `faction_strength` does not include militia or other clans of the kingdom. Town/castle counts describe the kingdom's holdings, matching the per-town bank and lord bonuses. Party averages count each active war party once, including army members; garrisons, caravans and villagers are excluded. `party_fill_percent` divides all field men by their combined capacity, so an oversized force can exceed 100%. Troop counts include wounded men and heroes; `field_healthy` separates ready men from wounded men. Numbers use invariant formatting for comparison across runs.

### Loss diagnostics

`Invasion battle:` records battle start/end, event ID, campaign day, battle type, settlement, both factions, winner, retreat and diplomatic termination. Each participant includes starting healthy men, killed/wounded/routed totals, remaining troops, current strength and non-hero troop counts by tier. Start/end snapshot labels distinguish troop composition before and after combat. A battle already underway when a save is loaded may only have an end entry.

`Invasion party observation:` records every active invasion field party, garrison and militia at load and daily: troop count/change, wounded, prisoners, tier mix, capacity, strength, food/change, starvation, morale, wages/unpaid wages, party/hero/clan gold, location, orders, army membership and battle ID. Daily deltas are explicitly **unattributed net changes**, not battle casualties: recruitment, reinforcement, transfers, starvation and combat can occur between observations. `Invasion desertion:` records actual deserter counts and troop IDs. `Invasion party loss:` identifies destruction/removal, the destroyer when available, and the last observation. Missing parties without a removal event are labeled `unknown_or_membership_changed`.

`Invasion lord event:` records capture, release, fugitive and death/removal details; `Invasion defeated lord removal:` explicitly identifies the mod's forced removal after defeat. These entries distinguish a native combat death from the invasion's defeat policy.

Capital objective logs include campaign day and a candidate roster with component, hero state, troop strength, current orders, battle/army membership and exact deferral reasons. Every existing objective reports daily even without a state transition, so stalled mobilization and dormant recovery remain visible. Diagnostics are observational, are rebuilt after load, and do not add saved state.

## Defeat removes the lord

A lord of a clan that is still fighting a live invasion is removed from the campaign the moment it is captured or becomes a fugitive, instead of waiting without a party until its clan's next daily tick hands it a fresh one. Capture, escape and ransom therefore all end the same way, and a ransom needs no handling of its own because the lord is already gone by the time one could be offered. The removal is `KillCharacterAction.ApplyByRemove`: the lord simply disappears, and no relation loss or honor penalty is charged to whoever beat it. `Hero.MainHero`, any hero of the player clan, and any hero whose clan is not fighting a live invasion are never touched.

The trigger is losing the invasion party, however that comes about, and not defeat as such: a party disbanding while stranded with no settlement in reach runs the same fugitive action a lost battle does, so that too removes its lord. A disband that reaches a settlement does not — it destroys the party without announcing a flight. Two shapes therefore escape in the other direction: that settled disband, and a battle ending in a retreat, which annihilates the losing party while marking its lord a fugitive silently. A lord lost either way reaches neither of the two events above and keeps the earlier behaviour of coming back with a fresh party.

Only a living adult lord is removed. An invasion may be configured against an existing clan rather than a spawned one, and retaking a town takes prisoner every hero of the owning clan residing in it, so the clan's spouses, children and quest heroes arrive at the same event a defeated lord does. The test is vanilla's own — the one it uses to pick a successor for a dead clan leader — because a lord's child carries the lord occupation from birth and an occupation alone would not tell the two apart.

Succession is the game's own. While another adult lord of the clan is alive, one is promoted and the clan keeps invading; the defeat that leaves no successor dissolves the clan, which is the invasion ending. How many defeats that takes is not the clan's configured lord count: a spawn that besieges settlements has a lord generated into its clan whenever it spawns without one, so a clan invading that way accumulates adult lords and outlasts correspondingly more defeats.

## Losing the foothold

Promotion hands an invasion to vanilla while its designated capital is held and not besieged. The first town captured establishes that capital. Losing it starts a capital recapture objective even if the invasion still holds another town. The capital's identity and the objective are saved; ownership and available forces are read from the live campaign, including on the first campaign tick after loading. A suspended invasion — removed configuration, or every one of its clans eliminated — never starts a new objective.

The founding clan identifies the invasion, but it does not have to survive it. When the founder dies out, the invasion passes to the clan of the invasion that rules the realm in its place, or else to any of its clans still alive, and carries on: its realm, culture and wars are read from that clan (`InvasionLeadership`). Before this, a founder killed in battle suspended the whole invasion while its vassal clans still held a town, so the invader neither relieved nor retook its capital.

The recapture objective mobilizes one native army and gathers near the target. Both native lord parties and registered custom invasion war parties can join, including leaderless escorts. The commander must have an active hero leading its own party; mobilization uses native army-creation requirements rather than the native strategic selector that excludes custom war parties. It prefers the nearest reachable village bound to a castle, with a town or castle as fallback. The rally point may belong to any faction: losing the kingdom's last fief must not prevent gathering. Gathering uses movement around that point rather than a patrol order, and only parties that have actually assembled count towards attack readiness. The army advances as soon as it meets both configured siege ratios: assembled strength against the target's garrison, militia and hostile occupants, and assembled strength against nearby hostile field forces. It does not wait for every invited party once those ratios pass.

If the lost capital remains too strong after three days of gathering, the objective excludes it and selects the closest alternative that is immediately viable under the original target-type and coastal rules. That alternative is attacked without another three-day wait. If none is viable, the objective becomes `Dormant/NoViableTarget`, releases its forced orders, and reconsiders once per day using current forces and targets. A newly viable target requires a unified army again before advancing. If an assigned attack target has no matching kingdom siege after seven days, the objective becomes `Dormant/AttackTimedOut`. A matching siege takes precedence at the deadline and is not abandoned because that deadline later passes. If the siege ends without capture, the objective becomes `Dormant/SiegeAborted` rather than being recreated hourly. All three dormant reasons reconsider current targets daily, including objectives restored from older saves. A dormant objective has no army, so a target is judged against the combined strength of the parties its next army would gather, not the commander's own party: judged by the commander alone, an invasion that still had towns and field parties never tried again after one failed siege. A viable target restarts mobilization with a fresh seven-day deadline; no viable target leaves the objective dormant.

Recapturing the designated capital completes the objective. Capturing a selected alternative while the designated capital is still lost makes that alternative the new capital and completes the objective. Holding another settlement alone does not relocate the capital. While the invasion still owns a besieged capital, its separate relief objective keeps gathering at the nearby rally point until it can relieve the siege or ownership changes; the three-day retarget and seven-day timeout apply to attacks, not to that defence wait.

A kingdom carrying a live invasion survives the loss of its last settlement for as long as the invasion can still retake its capital. `CanKingdomBeDiscontinuedEvent` is vetoed for any kingdom one of whose clans carries an unsuspended invasion that still has something to fight with and is either still landing (no capital yet) or has a capital objective that is not dormant. Once the attack on the lost capital has failed and gone dormant, the veto lifts and the landless remnant is retired as below, rather than wandering the map for months as a kingdom without a fief.

During the initial landing, the coastal restriction chooses and sizes up its target as one force rather than as whichever party happens to be deciding. Candidate towns are ranked by the summed gate distance of every member, and a garrison is weighed against the summed strength of those same members. Escorts are members themselves and are counted once. A capital recapture objective instead uses its commander's assembled army and selects alternatives by distance from that commander; it retains the invasion's configured coastal and target-type restrictions. Once a capital is held without a hostile siege, native AI resumes.

An invasion with no surviving member and no foothold is spent, and the kingdom it was given is retired; so is a landless kingdom whose invasion can no longer retake its capital. The veto stops applying at that point, but vanilla only asks whether a kingdom can be discontinued when a settlement changes hands or the player switches kingdom, so the missing trigger is supplied on the hourly tick. The rule that decides is vanilla's own — not eliminated, not the player kingdom, holding no settlement, and the event dispatcher consulted so other mods keep their vote — and so are the actions that end it: each clan leaves as an independent with its map and siege events finalised, the ruling clan is cleared, and the kingdom is destroyed. Departing clans then get the usual native time to survive on their own.

The end is deliberately gradual. Reinforcements need the capital itself, held and not under siege, so an invasion that has lost it draws none and can only bleed down while it fights to take it back. An invasion also loses the `BankedTroopsPerTownHeld` and `LordsPerTownHeld` share of every town its kingdom loses, so its bank and its lords shrink with its holdings and it cannot rebuild to what it had at its height. A bank held near its limit is also a capital its enemies can take, which is what finally ends it. A spent invasion is kept in state only until its kingdom has been retired, since it is what names that kingdom; the next reconciliation then drops it and cancels its remaining captures. Only the capital's identity is persisted: both the offensive switch and the spent condition are otherwise derived from live membership and footholds.

## Conquest and threat

An invasion at war with everyone fights nobody in particular: kingdoms at peace with each other pile onto whatever it took, and it ends up weakening the kingdom it landed in while the neighbours collect the fiefs. `Conquest` makes it a realm among realms instead. It stays at war with the kingdom it first took a fief from, its target, and every other kingdom treats it as it would any neighbour: vanilla decides their wars and peace, nothing is forced or forbidden. Minor factions and bandits are left to the diplomacy configuration throughout.

Two things keep that from being a quiet border war. The first is threat: each fief the invasion holds adds `ThreatPerFiefHeld` of the invasion's settlement worth to every kingdom's score for declaring war on it, and takes the same off their score for making peace. Worth grows with the fiefs too, so the threat grows faster than the holdings: a foothold is shrugged off and a realm is feared. The threat never overrides vanilla's own refusals, such as a realm too far away to reach, so only the invasion's neighbours feel it; one that lands in a remote corner can fight its target alone for a long time.

The second is opportunity. Once a day, an invasion whose fiefs can all hold (see `MinimumFiefGarrison`) looks at the fief nearest to its own. When that fief belongs to a kingdom it is not at war with, it declares war on that kingdom rather than march past it, at most once every `OpenFrontIntervalInDays`. Each new front adds to the threat, so the invasion can grow in any direction, but every step it takes raises the number of kingdoms ready to fight it. The log records `Invasion conquest: front opened`, `Invasion threat: war` with the threat each side saw, and a daily `Invasion threat:` line with the invasion's fiefs, threat, war threshold and wars.

New fronts do not pull the invasion's lords away from the kingdom it is conquering. Every hour each lord weighs the sieges it could lay, as vanilla lords do, and `TargetSiegePreference` multiplies its wish to besiege a fief of the target kingdom; a siege of that kingdom vanilla judges can be won is wanted above every other siege. Vanilla only weighs a siege of a fief that has fortifications of the lord's realm among its neighbours, so a fief of the target kingdom beyond the invasion's border is never besieged. While no fief of the target is worth a siege, such a fief is offered too: wanted above every other siege, if vanilla judges it can be taken. Vanilla does not weigh such a siege once it has begun either, so the lord besieging it is offered it again each hour, weighed with everyone in its camp; without that the lord would leave the camp on its next thought. A daily `Invasion unbordered siege:` line per such fief shows how it was weighed. Vanilla's military AI is registered again on the first campaign tick, so that it weighs each hour before these preferences reweigh it, since the newest listener of a campaign event is called first. That kingdom is finished first, while the other wars are fought in the field and at their borders. Once it falls the preference ends with the conquest.

While an invasion is being fought, its spawn rolls no new force: reinforcements reach it only through its capital. A new force can land again once the invasion has ended.

Opportunity widens the war, but it does not decide where the invasion's own armies march. When the capital's army picks a fief to besiege, any viable fief of the target comes before a closer one of anyone else's, so the target falls instead of being left with a handful of fiefs while the invasion spreads past it. Only when none of the target's fiefs can be taken right now does the army go for the closest one it can, whoever holds it. Once the target has fallen, the closest viable fief is the only rule.

`MinimumFiefGarrison` is the other half: an invasion that takes fiefs faster than it can garrison them loses them one after another. The capital's bank tops up each held fief before the lords may lay a new siege, so the invasion's reach grows with its waves instead of past them.

Vanilla sizes a garrison's wage budget and party size for a kingdom's own levies. An invader's tier 4 and 5 troops cost several times as much, so a topped-up garrison deserted up to 20 men a day for wages, and a capital bank over its party size limit lost a quarter of the excess every day; in one 370-day run that drained 11,000 men from the capital and 4,500 from a single castle, which the top-ups kept refilling. `InvasionPartyDesertionModel` therefore spares the garrisons of a live invasion's fiefs vanilla's wage and party-size desertion. A garrison whose morale breaks still deserts, though the crowding penalty vanilla takes off a party over its size limit is not counted (the capital banks its reserve far past that limit by design; in one run crowding alone took the bank to 18 morale and a single setback then cost it 396 men), so does one whose clan cannot pay it (five men a day, as in vanilla), and so does every field party. Each day a garrison is spared or unpaid, `Invasion garrison desertion:` logs its wage, payment limit, owner gold, unpaid wages, what vanilla would have deserted and what actually deserted. The bank limit remains the bank's only valve.

The bank does not eat the capital's food either. Vanilla feeds every garrison member from the town's stocks, one food a day per twenty men, so a 3,563-man bank ate 178 a day. The town starved between convoys, the starving garrison lay wounded and loyalty fell, until the militia outnumbered the garrison still standing by 1.4 to 1 and the town rebelled, and the rebels took the whole reserve. `InvasionSettlementFoodModel` adds back the food eaten by the men above an invader-held garrison's party size limit (`Invasion supply train` in the food tooltip); the men within the limit eat as any garrison does.

An invasion never stops conquering. When its target kingdom falls, the invasion turns on the next: of the kingdoms holding a fortification that neighbours one of its fiefs, the best prey: the most such fortifications for its total strength, since an invader that picks a fight with a stronger neighbour is beaten back before it can grow, counted twice over for a kingdom it already fights and again for one an ally conquers, so a line finishes kingdoms together (`Invasion conquest: retargeted`, which lists every neighbour's strength, border, coast and war). With `ReconsiderTargetIntervalInDays` set, the invasion weighs its target the same way every so often, as wars move borders: the target may withdraw behind fortifications out of reach while another kingdom takes the fiefs next to the invasion's. It turns on a neighbour once that one is half again the better prey, and a target no longer on its borders counts as no prey at all (`Invasion conquest: reconsidered`). A realm that has joined its line's newest realm on that one's target does not reconsider while it is joined, so it does not turn away from the front it came to help. Turning away launches no follower: only a fallen kingdom does. The new target's war is declared the same day, and its fiefs take the siege preference. With no kingdom on its borders the invasion has nothing to turn on (`Invasion conquest: no neighbour to turn on`), and it tries again the next day. Reinforcements, top-ups, threat and the influence-free armies of its clans go on for as long as it fights, so clans the invasion founds can still raise armies of their own.

Every kingdom an invasion conquers opens a new invasion realm. When the target of an invasion falls, or the target of any realm launched for it, the line launches a follower. The next follower configured for it (`FollowsInvasion`) that is not already in the field and whose clan still stands goes first, in configuration order. Once there is none, the line launches a copy of its first invasion instead, as often as its kingdoms fall: the same spawn, with the same settings, party template, escorts and spawn message, fighting for a clan founded for it. That clan is named after its culture and landing town and takes the id of the first invasion's clan plus `_line_1`, `_line_2` and so on. It has the first invasion's culture and tier, a random banner whose colours become the realm's, and a new lord at its head. It joins no kingdom, and founds its own with the first fief it takes, as a configured follower does. It inherits the diplomacy configured for the first invasion's clan, `ForceNoKingdom` and forced wars included. A line therefore opens a new realm with every kingdom it conquers, without limit. A kingdom that several realms of the line conquer together falls for all of them on the same day, yet launches only one follower: the first realm to see it fall claims the fall. The follower lands in the coastal town of a standing kingdom lying farthest from every fief its line holds, so it opens a war in another part of the map; a kingdom one of the line already conquers is passed over while any other has a coast. The kingdom it lands in becomes its own target, and it declares war on it (`Invasion conquest: launched`, which logs the landing town and its distance, `clone=True` and the clan's name for a copy, or `launch failed`; `no follower` when the line's first invasion is gone from the field or from the configuration). A copy whose clan cannot be founded or whose spawn fails launches nothing, and its clan is destroyed again. Until it holds a fief of its own, its first landing is restricted to the target's coast. Each realm has its own capital, bank, waves, lords and target, but the line converges on the kingdom its newest realm conquers: the newest follower opens the front and keeps its own target, and every older realm of the line, the leader and earlier followers alike, turns on that kingdom while the newest conquers it, however far it lies (`Invasion conquest: joined`, naming the target it left and the follower it joined); their lords are offered its fiefs beyond their borders as any target's, and the war on it is declared the same day. A realm whose newest follower has no standing target, or whose line has none, retargets as any invasion does when its target falls. The whole line, the leader and every follower launched for it, is kept at peace and, once each rules a kingdom, allied through vanilla's alliance (`Invasion alliance: peace`, `Invasion alliance: allied`); followers stay allied with each other after the leader falls. The diplomacy configuration cannot set them at war, and none counts another's realm as a neighbour to turn on; a save whose invasion already targets an ally retargets on the first day. If the leader falls, a follower whose target falls launches the next follower itself, allied with that follower only. A copy shares its first invasion's spawn template, so the line tells its realms apart by clan: a copy that loses track of its conquest picks the alliance up again with the invasion it was launched for and no other, and a configured follower never takes a copy of its leader for the leader. While any realm of the line runs the first invasion's spawn, that spawn is not rolled again by chance.

It fights for as long as it has a realm. Once its capital is taken, that realm is a kingdom one of its clans rules; when the realm itself falls (`Invasion fallen`), the invasion is suspended for good. The clans it expanded into are left to vanilla, which may take them into another kingdom, but the invasion does not count that kingdom's fiefs, bank against them, or keep its conquest going on their behalf.

A big bank sitting in the capital wins no sieges, so with `GarrisonArmy` set the invasion leads part of it out. Once a day each fighting invasion that holds its capital is looked at; once `IntervalInDays` have passed since it last raised one (the first is due at once) and its last army is no longer in the field, it raises one unless the capital is besieged, the garrison holds no men above `MinimumCapitalGarrison`, the target kingdom has no fortification left or is at peace with it, or no lord could be made to lead it (`Invasion garrison army: skipped`, with `reason=capital_besieged`, `garrison_at_minimum`, `no_target`, `no_lord` or `army_refused`, logged once until the reason changes). Each army gets a lord of its own: a new lord of the clan holding the capital, made only for it. He counts for nothing against the invasion's lord cap, which grows only with the towns it takes. His party is fielded at the capital with all the army's men drawn from the garrison and a month of grain (or vanilla's minimum for an army, if longer); its size limit is exactly those men, and it loses none to wage or over-limit desertion. He then leads a vanilla besieger army, a kingdom army like any other, against the target kingdom's fortification nearest the capital, and every lord of the invasion's clans free to answer, wherever he is, is called to it; others may join it later the usual way (`Invasion garrison army: raised`, with leader, men, lords called, garrison left and target; `Invasion garrison army: gathered`, with the lords and men it gathered). It costs no influence and never loses cohesion. It fights on until its lord is beaten: once a fortification falls it moves on to the nearest fortification of the kingdom the invasion conquers then, following the conquest to its next kingdom, and with nothing to besiege it defends the capital (or the realm's nearest fortification) rather than idling, which vanilla would punish by breaking it up. When its lord is captured, killed or loses his army, whatever men his party still has go straight back into the capital's garrison, or, with the capital lost, into the realm's nearest fortification, prisoners into its dungeon, and the lord is removed for good, as a captive too, so no ransom is offered (`Invasion garrison army: disbanded`, with the reason, the men returned and `lord_removed`). Vanilla can still break the army up for starvation or a war with no fief-holding enemy left; its reason is logged and the army is ended the same way. Each invasion's last raise day is saved under `_invasionGarrisonArmies`, and the lord in the field under `_invasionGarrisonArmyLords`, so after a load he is still known, kept out of the lord cap and removed when his army ends; a save from before the feature raises its first army on the first day.

The conquest is saved under its own key, `_invasionConquests`, as the invasion's clan id against its target, start day, the day it last opened a front, and the clan ids of its leader and of the follower it last launched, which a save from before followers lacks. A save from before the feature has no conquest, so the next fief the invasion takes names its target. A save that marked the conquest settled names a fallen target, so the invasion turns on its next one on the first day. Copies are saved under `_invasionLineClans`, each copy's clan id against the clan of the invasion it was launched for and the clan it was modelled on; their diplomacy is inherited again on load. The copy's clan, its invasion and its conquest are saved as any other's, and the invasion clan repository maps the copy's clan to the first invasion's spawn template.

## Save compatibility and verification

Existing save IDs and original-spawn meanings are retained. Invasion, role, foothold, capture evidence, occupation completion and scheduling data are appended. Legacy saves adopt identifiable parties prospectively, without inferring old conquests from clan ownership. Ambiguous capture history gets no retroactive troop transfer. Removed configuration or eliminated clans suspend new effects while allowing saved state to be cleaned up.

Compile references remain v1.4.7; installed v1.4.8 is the accepted native verification runtime. Focused tests establish rule, mapping and adapter-boundary behaviour, not live campaign balance. The bank limit and wave cap are exercised against plain numbers only: what a template roll actually puts into a garrison, are reachable only through the adapter and are not pinned by a test. Before treating sample balance as verified, record a new campaign and pre-feature-save load, occupation/deadline reloads, and at least three weekly cycles with loyalty, security, militia, garrison strength, donor counts/XP, clan wages and party counts — including one cycle with a full bank, to confirm the wave is refused and sent later rather than lost, and one with the capital besieged, to confirm neither a wave in nor a lord's draw out.

Runtime verification status and exact build/test evidence belong in the implementation report. Do not infer a successful live playtest from this configuration example.
